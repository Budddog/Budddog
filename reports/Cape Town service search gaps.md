# Cheap clicks favour cleaning over plumbing

The best-supported candidate for a "capture on Google, hand the work to someone else" business in Cape Town is **specialist cleaning (deep, move-out, Airbnb turnover and carpet cleaning)**, followed by tree felling, waterproofing and damp proofing, gate and garage motor repair, and aircon installation. This ranking is an inference, not a measurement: the researchers found **no measured Google search volume for any Cape Town or South African service keyword**, could not view live Google South Africa results, and read most sources only as search-result summaries. What the evidence does show is a consistent pattern. The categories with the most demand (plumbing, electrical, locksmiths, solar) are also the ones agencies quote at the highest click prices, R20 to R78, and they carry licensing rules that complicate subcontracting. Cleaning sits third in national marketplace demand at a proxy click cost near R12, needs no licence that the research could find, and has an abundant pool of people to do the work. The second finding matters as much as the ranking: at South African prices, buying clicks and reselling leads barely breaks even, so the model only works on jobs worth several thousand rand, on free organic traffic, or where the owner invoices the customer and keeps the full margin. Everything below should be treated as a shortlist to test with a Keyword Planner export and a manual check of live results, both specified at the end.

## What this research could and could not measure

Three limits shape every conclusion here. First, **no keyword-level search volumes were obtained**. Keyword tools (Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush) could not be queried, and the public keyword tables that surfaced were US data, which is unusable for Cape Town. The demand evidence is therefore a proxy: Procompare's analysis of more than 100,000 service requests submitted across South Africa in 2024, with no Western Cape breakdown ([Procompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/pro-info-centre/trends/services-in-demand)), plus local demand drivers such as storms, tourism and sewer blockages.

Second, **no live Google South Africa result page was seen**. The search tool used was a US-based web index that shows no ads, no map pack and no Google Business Profile review counts. Statements about who appears for a query describe what that index returned, for only eight categories with one query each. No advertiser counts or map-pack data exist for any category.

Third, **direct page fetches were blocked**, so nearly every figure comes from a search-result summary rather than a full read of the source. Several numbers could not be tied cleanly to one page, and those are either omitted or flagged. Click costs are agency planning bands and category averages, not keyword-level bids, and they come from firms that sell Google Ads management. In this report, "sourced" means a figure reported by a linked source, "derived" means arithmetic on sourced figures, and "estimate" means the researchers' or this writer's judgement.

## Demand is highest where clicks cost the most

The marketplace proxy puts **plumbers first, building contractors second, cleaning services third, solar fourth and accountants fifth** by 2024 request volume, with requests growing fastest for air conditioning (+156% year on year), plumbers (+73%), building contractors (+23%) and electricians (+21%) ([Procompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/pro-info-centre/trends/services-in-demand)). Cape Town's own conditions reinforce the plumbing signal: the City attended 110,598 sewer blockages in 2024/25, one every eight minutes ([City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/Media-and-news/Sewer%20blockages%20cleared%20every%20eight%20minutes%20costs%20City%20millions)).

Click prices rise with urgency and ticket size, and the high-demand trades sit at the top. One agency estimates a "plumber" keyword at **R20 to R30 in Cape Town** against R30 to R45 in Johannesburg ([New Age Marketing](https://newagemarketing.co.za/how-google-ads-pricing-works/)). Another puts emergency trades at R25 to R65, local emergency services including locksmiths at R25 to R55, and solar at R35 to R78 ([Moonlit](https://www.moonlit.co.za/blog/google-ads-cost-south-africa-pricing-guide/)). Lower down, an agency's own account data gives category averages of **R12.42 for consumer services, R13.27 for construction and trades**, R17.44 for auto repairs and R19.96 for renewable energy ([BaseCloud](https://basecloudglobal.com/google-ads-benchmarks-for-south-african-industries/)). A Cape Town agency applies the R12.42 figure to cleaning while stating that no public benchmark reports cleaning click costs directly ([Growth Pulse Media](https://www.growthpulsemedia.co.za/google-ads-for-cleaning-services-south-africa/)).

These numbers deserve suspicion. Published South African averages range from R4 to R6 per click ([AdBot](https://adbot.co.za/how-to-advertise-online/google-ads-benchmarks-for-your-sa-industry-2025/)) to R88.97 ([Juicy Designs](https://www.juicydesigns.co.za/research/south-african-search-advertising-benchmarks-2026/)), and quotes for the single word "plumber" run from about R2 ([Deep Thought Media](https://deepthoughtmedia.co.za/google-ads-cape-town/)) to R65. Only the relative ordering is trustworthy: emergency and regulated trades cost the most, and non-urgent consumer and building services cost the least. For most of the long tail (pest control, tree felling, rubble removal, gate motors, pool and garden services, aircon, appliance repair) **no South African click cost was found at all**.

Local drivers point to where demand is building beyond the national ranking. Cape Town has **26,877 Airbnb listings**, 5,662 of them in one inner-city ward ([GroundUp](https://groundup.news/article/inner-city-cape-town-no-room-for-locals/)), and Western Cape rents rose 9.7% in a year ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/18/cape-town-rents-jump-by-more-than-r1-100-in-a-year)), both of which feed turnover and end-of-lease cleaning. May 2026 storms uprooted trees and stripped roofs across the metro ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-05-11-cold-fronts-lash-western-cape-one-dead-communities-flooded-and-schools-shut/)), insurers warned that claims fail where poor roof, gutter and tree maintenance contributed ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/05/22/as-wc-storm-insurance-claims-surge-insurers-spotlight-house-maintenance)), and the provincial fire season was extended from four to eight months ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/08/24/western-cape-government-expands-fire-season-from-four-to-eight-months)). Solar runs the other way: residential demand is down an estimated 65 to 80% from its 2023 peak ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/02/20/south-africa-adds-1-6-gw-of-solar-in-2025)) while clicks remain among the dearest.

## Reselling leads barely pays, so ticket size decides

Cape Town contractors already buy leads, and the going rate is low. Snupit charges R24 to R30 per credit and 1 to 5 credits per lead ([Snupit](https://www.snupit.co.za/faq-pro.aspx)), and Procompare reportedly charges R40 to R150 per lead, shared with up to five competing professionals ([Procompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/pro-info-centre/success-tips/how-procompare-works)). That **R24 to R150 shared-lead price is the anchor** any lead seller will be compared against. No source quantified a premium for exclusive leads or a standard referral commission.

Against that anchor, paid clicks look expensive. No South African conversion benchmark for trades was found, so the only option is to borrow US figures: 7.33% of home-services ad clicks become leads ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-advertising-benchmarks/)), and about 41.5% of plumbing leads become booked jobs ([ClicksGeek](https://clicksgeek.com/google-ads-performance-benchmarks-plumbing/)). Applied to a South African agency's R8 to R15 click estimate for plumbing and electrical keywords ([RaimondWeb](https://raimondweb.pages.dev/insights/seo-vs-google-ads-south-africa)), the derived cost is **R110 to R205 per lead and R265 to R495 per booked job**. A lead that costs R110 to R205 to generate cannot be resold profitably at R40 to R150. Recovering R265 to R495 from a 10 to 15% commission (an assumed rate) requires a job worth roughly R1,800 to R5,000.

Three consequences follow. Small tickets fail on paid traffic: a Cape Town lockout at R450 to R900 ([Cape Town Locksmiths](https://cptlocksmiths.co.za/pricing)), a garden visit at R400 to R600 ([Procompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/garden-maintenance)) or a bakkie load of rubble at R300 to R450 ([Kandua](https://kandua.com/-/cost/cost-of-construction-rubble-removal-in-south-africa)) leaves no room unless the customer recurs. Mid tickets clear the bar: a deep clean at R2,500 to R3,500 ([Procompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/articles/waterproofing/how-much-does-deep-cleaning-cost)), tree felling at R1,200 to R6,500 per tree ([Completo](https://www.completo.co.za/prices/landscaping-costs)), a gate motor at R6,500 to R12,000 installed ([Kandua](https://kandua.com/cost-guides/garage-gate-motor-installation-cost-in-south-africa-automating-your-access-for-convenience-security)) or a geyser replacement at R7,000 to R15,000 ([Kandua](https://kandua.com/home-advice/how-much-does-it-cost-to-replace-a-geyser-in-south-africa-2026)). And organic ranking changes the sums entirely, which is why thin competition matters more than cheap clicks.

Regulation then sorts the mid-ticket list. Only a registered person with a wireman's licence can issue an electrical certificate of compliance ([Empowr](https://www.empowr.co.za/compliance/wiremans-licence)), geyser installations need a certificate from a PIRB-registered plumber ([Kwikot](https://www.kwikot.com/geyser-complaince-in-south-africa)), and pest control operators must be registered under Act 36 of 1947 ([SME South Africa](https://smesouthafrica.co.za/how-to-start-a-pest-control-business)). None of this stops a non-tradesperson owning the customer, but the subcontractor must hold the registration and sign in their own name. Security work is riskier: a procurement blog lists alarm, CCTV and locksmith services under the PSIRA regulator, which governs the business providing the service and so could capture the front company itself ([Tenders-SA](https://www.tenders-sa.org/blog/security-procurement-guide-2026-03-26)); this needs legal confirmation. Anyone transporting waste in Cape Town must be accredited by the City ([City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/City-Connect/Register/Business-and-trade/Register-as-an-accredited-waste-services-provider)), which puts rubble removal and skip hire in the regulated column too. The research found no licence requirement for cleaning, carpet cleaning, garden or pool services, painting, paving, tiling, handyman work, damp proofing or waterproofing, which is an absence of evidence and not legal clearance.

Two structural rules apply to every category. Google's Business Profile guidelines exclude virtual offices ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)), and third-party summaries say lead-generation companies are ineligible ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/representing-your-business-on-google/)), so **a map listing needs a real operating business**; a pure lead seller is confined to ads and organic results. And under section 54 of the Consumer Protection Act the supplier owes the customer quality service, with redo or refund as the remedy ([CPA s54](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/54_consumer_s_rights_to_demand_quality_service.php)). An owner who invoices in their own name keeps the margin and, on the researchers' reading, the liability; an owner who only sells leads sheds the liability and most of the margin.

**Specialist cleaning** is the only category with evidence on all four tests. The same agency that supplies the R12.42 proxy calculates a planning cost of about R116 per lead from it ([Growth Pulse Media](https://www.growthpulsemedia.co.za/google-ads-for-cleaning-services-south-africa/)); at the borrowed 41.5% booking rate that is roughly R280 per booked job (derived), about a tenth of a deep-clean ticket. The category to avoid within it is routine domestic cleaning, where SweepSouth advertises from R29 an hour ([SweepSouth](https://sweepsouth.com/locations/western-cape/cape-town)) and sets a price floor no middleman can sit above. Demand is also seasonal, with Procompare reporting a peak-to-trough ratio of about 3.5 for cleaning.

**Tree felling** ranks second on hand-off fit and weak web supply, despite having no volume or click-cost figure. Eight of 18 results for the query were Procompare provider profiles, and the rest included Google Sites pages and keyword pages hosted on Amazon S3 ([example](https://cape-town-tree-felling.s3.amazonaws.com/cape-town-tree-felling.html)). The City requires a qualified person only for protected species ([City of Cape Town](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Bylaws%20and%20policies/Urban_Forest%20Policy.pdf)). **Waterproofing and damp proofing** has the largest unregulated tickets, with roof waterproofing at R100 to R180 per square metre ([Completo](https://www.completo.co.za/prices/waterproofing-costs)) and one user-submitted damp-proofing estimate of R42,500 for 170 square metres ([Procompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/providers/damp-control-cape-cc-1)). It ranks below tree felling because established contractors with suburb landing pages already appear ([Wilcote](https://wilcotecapetown.co.za/roofing-ceiling-and-damp-proofing-services-in-cape-town)). The one South African lead-generation artefact found sits in this space: a roofing site listed for sale claiming 90 to 120 organic enquiries a month, with the profit field blank ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/es/13916033-south-african-roofing-lead-gen-site-ranking-1-for-key-terms-and-generating-90-120-organic-roofing-enquiries-monthly-with-no-paid-ads)). Sold once each at R40 to R150, those enquiries would gross R3,600 to R18,000 a month, which is arithmetic on an unverified seller claim.

**Gate motor repair** earns fourth place almost entirely on supply-side weakness: of 17 results returned, none was an independent business website, only Procompare profiles, Kandua suburb pages such as [Durbanville](https://kandua.com/gate-motor-repairs/durbanville), Gumtree adverts and one directory listing. **Aircon** has the strongest growth signal and the least supporting data. **Plumbing-adjacent work** has the most demand of anything on the list and a healthy ticket, but it is the textbook expensive click, and one Cape Town plumbing firm displays 1,235 collected reviews ([Trustindex](https://www.trustindex.io/reviews/www.absoluteplumbingcapetown.co.za/lang/en)), a hint of how far ahead incumbents are.

The categories to leave alone are clearer than the ranking of the winners. **Locksmiths** combine a top-band click price with a sub-R1,000 daytime ticket and security-industry regulation. **Solar** pairs the dearest trade clicks with collapsed demand and a City rule that systems be authorised with professional sign-off ([City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/applyforsseg)). Borehole drilling, towing and septic pumping depend on owners of expensive equipment who have little reason to pay a small middleman (estimate); septic pumping returned the emptiest results of any query, but the City runs its own tanker service and demand is confined to unsewered areas. Rubble removal, appliance repair and mobile car washing are low-ticket and already crowded with throwaway lead-generation pages, including matching "Pros Cape Town" listings for [rubble removal](https://www.bizcommunity.com/Company/RubbleRemovalProsCapeTown) and [appliance repair](https://www.bizcommunity.com/Company/ApplianceRepairProsCapeTown).

One caution applies to every "weak competition" reading above. Aggregators already publish templated pages by service and suburb, so the long tail is contested even where local businesses are absent. And if secondary sources are right that Google's pay-per-lead Local Services Ads are not offered in South Africa ([Juicy Designs](https://www.juicydesigns.co.za/blog/google-ads-vs-local-services-ads-south-africa/)), ordinary search ads, the map pack and organic results are the whole battlefield.

## Four datasets will confirm or kill this shortlist

**A Google Keyword Planner export is the first requirement**, because it fills both empty columns at once. Set the location to Cape Town (and separately to Western Cape), language to English, and pull average monthly searches, the twelve-month trend, the competition index, and top-of-page bid low and high for roughly 40 keywords. Cover each shortlisted category in three forms: "[service] cape town", "[service] near me", and two or three suburb variants such as Durbanville, Somerset West, Sea Point and Table View. Include the full shortlist (deep cleaning, move out cleaning, Airbnb cleaning, carpet cleaning, tree felling, waterproofing, damp proofing, roof repairs, gate motor repairs, garage door repairs, aircon installation, aircon repair, geyser replacement, blocked drains, painters, paving, pool cleaning, garden services, pest control) and the expected losers (plumber, electrician, locksmith, solar installation, rubble removal, appliance repair) as controls. Low-volume keywords will come back in coarse ranges unless the account has active spend, so a small live campaign or a paid tool with a South African database gives sharper numbers. Divide monthly searches by the top-of-page high bid to get the ratio this report could not compute.

**A manual check of live results is the second.** From a device physically in Cape Town, or with location set to specific suburbs, search each shortlisted head term in a private window and record: how many ads appear above the organic results; the three map-pack businesses with their review counts and ratings; which of the first ten organic results are aggregators (Procompare, Kandua, Snupit, Gumtree), free-hosted pages or real local companies; and whether those company sites look current. Repeat for two suburb variants per category, since map results depend on proximity. Google's Ads Transparency Center will show which advertisers are active per category. A category is underserved if the map pack is filled by profiles with few reviews and the organic page is mostly directories.

**Third, verify the sources that were only read as summaries.** Open the Procompare demand page for absolute request counts and any city split, the Procompare and Snupit pricing pages for current lead prices, Google's Local Services Ads country list, and the Business Profile guidelines on lead-generation eligibility. Run a Google Trends comparison for the shortlist restricted to the Western Cape over five years to confirm seasonality and direction.

**Fourth, test the supply side directly.** Phone five to ten subcontractors in each of the top three categories and ask what they pay for leads now, whether they would pay more for an exclusive phone-qualified lead, and what commission or wholesale rate they would accept on white-labelled jobs. Confirm with the relevant bodies whether a front company needs its own registration for security work, waste transport and aircon refrigerant handling. Only then run a two-week test campaign of a few thousand rand in the top two categories to replace the borrowed US conversion rates with real ones.

## Conclusion

The question assumed that the prize is a category with high volume and cheap clicks. The evidence suggests that the pairing is rare by construction, because click prices follow urgency and job value, and that it is not the binding constraint. What separates workable categories from unworkable ones in Cape Town is whether the job is large enough to absorb a customer-acquisition cost of several hundred rand, and whether anyone can legally and reliably do the work under someone else's brand. On those tests the unglamorous middle wins: jobs worth R2,500 to R15,000 that need no certificate, in categories where the current competition is a directory profile or a free-hosted page.

The shortlist also implies a business model. Reselling shared leads at marketplace prices is a thin business on paid traffic, so the owner either invoices the customer directly and manages a subcontractor, accepting the liability that comes with it, or builds organic rankings patiently and sells exclusive leads at a premium the market has not yet priced. Cape Town's demand drivers favour the first route in cleaning, where Airbnb turnover gives recurring work, and the second in storm-driven trades, where demand arrives in seasonal bursts. A week of Keyword Planner and live-result checks will show whether the five leading candidates hold up, and that check costs far less than building for a category chosen on proxies.
