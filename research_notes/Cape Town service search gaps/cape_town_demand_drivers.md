## 1. Semigration and property boom

### Takeaway
Cape Town remains SA's strongest housing market (house prices +9-11% y/y, rents +~10% y/y in 2026) and still the top destination for net homeowner inflow, but semigration has cooled from its 2022 peak and an affordability-driven "reverse semigration" narrative is now established. Demand for move-in/turnover services is sustained by price growth and churn rather than by an accelerating inflow.

### Cited Findings
- Lightstone town-level data: Cape Town leads net homeowner gain with "over 1,500 homeowners"; 14 of the top 15 net-gain towns are in the Western Cape (dataset date not confirmed in snippet) — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/property/794873/major-semigration-shift-in-south-africa-these-are-the-towns-people-are-flocking-to/)
- Lightstone data showed a drop-off in Western Cape semigration in H1 2024 versus the 2022 peak — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/property/806888/goodbye-cape-town-new-semigration-shift-hitting-south-africa/)
- "Reverse semigration": some former Western Cape residents returning to Gauteng, attributed to Cape Town living costs (BetterBond commentary) — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/lifestyle/814446/goodbye-cape-town-reverse-semigration-trend-hitting-south-africa/); [Business Day, 3 Jul 2025](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/property/2025-07-03-joburg-lures-back-semigrants-as-cape-towns-cost-squeeze-bites/)
- Conflicting signal: Business Day (Jul 2025) cited Lightstone as showing net migration to the Western Cape up 22.5% y/y — [Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/property/2025-07-03-joburg-lures-back-semigrants-as-cape-towns-cost-squeeze-bites/); contradicted in direction by the slowdown reports above (probably different measures/periods; unresolved).
- Average property sale price rose from R1.6m (2020) to R2.1m (2025) per Lightstone (snippet did not make clear whether Cape Town or Western Cape) — source among the semigration articles above, most likely [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/property/869720/bad-news-for-anyone-buying-a-house-in-cape-town/) (attribution not verified).
- Western Cape house price growth averaged 10.6% in the year to July 2026 vs 3% Gauteng and 2.6% KZN; Lightstone puts Cape Town at 11.4% vs Johannesburg 2.3% — [Pam Golding Properties](https://www.pamgolding.co.za/news-insights/sa-s-housing-market-shows-resilience-as-demand-shifts-towards-value-and-opportunity)
- Alternative figure: Western Cape annual house price growth 9.3% vs Gauteng 3.8% — [MyProperty, Mar 2026](https://www.myproperty.co.za/news/market-and-opinion/western-cape-set-to-remain-south-africa-s-strongest-property-market-in-2026-09-03-26)
- Western Cape average price in Q1 2026 was ~72% above the national average; average deposit rose to 13.4% of purchase price in Q2 2026; Western Cape prices +7.5% y/y in Dec 2025 — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/property/869720/bad-news-for-anyone-buying-a-house-in-cape-town/) and [IOL, 2 Jul 2026](https://iol.co.za/business/property/2026-07-02-cape-town-is-winning-on-lifestyle-but-losing-homebuyers-on-affordability/) (which figure sits in which article not verified)
- Gauteng still accounts for 50.8% of national property transfers by volume — [Business Report, 18 Mar 2026](https://businessreport.co.za/business/property/2026-03-18-south-africas-2025-property-market-defies-norms-premium-segments-lead-activity)
- PayProp Q2 2026: Western Cape average monthly rent R12,561, +9.7% y/y; Cape Town rents up R1,107 in a year; PayProp's Michelle Dickens cites semigration/population growth as the driver — [EWN, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/18/cape-town-rents-jump-by-more-than-r1-100-in-a-year)
- Cape Town's population could grow by up to 2.9 million by 2050 — [Cape Argus/IOL, 29 Aug 2025](https://iol.co.za/capeargus/news/2025-08-29-what-cape-towns-expected-population-growth-of-2-9-million-by-2050-means-for-the-city/)

### Inferences
- High prices plus high deposits mean buyers are relatively affluent and likely to outsource: pre-sale/post-purchase painting, renovations (kitchens/bathrooms), handyman, deep/move-in cleaning, garden services, security installs, compliance certificates (electrical, plumbing, gas, electric fence, beetle - all required on transfer in Cape Town; not sourced here).
- Fast rent growth (+~10%) and tenant churn feed end-of-lease cleaning, carpet cleaning, small removals/"bakkie" movers, and landlord maintenance.
- Two-way flows (in-migration and reverse semigration) both generate long-distance removals (Cape Town-Gauteng) and storage demand.

- No Cape Town-specific building-plan/renovation statistics found (City plan approvals, Stats SA building statistics not retrieved).
- No data on removals-company volumes.

## 2. Short-term rental / Airbnb market

### Takeaway
Cape Town has roughly 26,000-27,000 Airbnb listings (up from ~23,500), heavily concentrated in the inner city/Atlantic Seaboard, and the City published a draft Short-Term Letting By-law (Aug 2026) requiring registration of every listed property. This is a large, dense, regulated-soon base for turnover cleaning, linen/laundry, key handover, maintenance and compliance services.

### Cited Findings
- 26,877 Airbnb listings in Cape Town, 5,662 of them in Ward 115 (inner city) — [GroundUp, Nov 2025](https://groundup.news/article/inner-city-cape-town-no-room-for-locals/) (also syndicated on [News24](https://www.news24.com/business/economy/inner-city-cape-town-no-room-for-locals-20251119-0719))
- ~26,304 active listings as of 25 June 2025; earlier Inside Airbnb count 23,564; Airbnb's own report counts ~7,362 listings rented for 90+ days/year (the "dedicated" stock) — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/cape-town-airbnb-boom-how-short-term-rentals-are-reshaping-the-city-housing-market-510000a) / [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/work-visas/1698586/a-critical-overview-of-the-sharing-economy-through-the-short-term-rental-regulatory-landscape) (which of the two carries each number not verified)
- City of Cape Town published a draft Short-Term Letting By-law for public comment (dated Aug 2026): all properties listed on any booking platform must register with the City and display a registration number; no night cap — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/trending/869234/new-laws-for-airbnbs-and-other-short-term-rentals-in-cape-town/); [Daily Investor](https://dailyinvestor.com/property/130116/cape-town-to-crack-down-on-airbnbs-and-other-short-term-rentals/)
- Tourism Minister Patricia de Lille (Jan 2026) linked unchecked short-term rental growth to Cape Town's inflated rents; national short-term letting regulation still absent as of Mar 2026 — [Property Professional, 16 Mar 2026](https://propertyprofessional.co.za/2026/03/16/the-airbnb-debate-regulation-reality-and-what-comes-next/)
- Existing framework: 2019 municipal planning by-law amendment permits short-term letting in general-residential zoned property; one source says max 30 days per stay (sources conflict on detail) — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/work-visas/1698586/a-critical-overview-of-the-sharing-economy-through-the-short-term-rental-regulatory-landscape)

### Inferences
- ~26k listings with frequent turnovers imply very high recurring demand for Airbnb cleaning, linen hire/laundry, restocking, check-in/key handover, co-hosting/management, plus fast-response handyman, plumbing, appliance repair, pest control and Wi-Fi/lock installs. Concentration in Ward 115/CBD, Sea Point, Green Point makes route-dense micro-operators viable.
- Registration requirement (if adopted) likely creates a new niche: "Airbnb registration/compliance" help (zoning checks, fire/safety compliance, body-corporate consent).
- Regulatory tightening could push some hosts to mid/long-term letting, shifting rather than removing cleaning/maintenance demand.

### Gaps
- Whether the draft by-law has been adopted and its final terms/fees: not confirmed.
- No AirDNA occupancy/revenue figures retrieved; no data on number of Airbnb management/cleaning companies or their quality.

## 3. Energy and water

### Takeaway
Load shedding has effectively ended (a full year without it by May 2026), and residential solar demand has fallen 65-80% from the 2023 peak; the energy niche has shifted from emergency backup to tariff-driven savings, compliance (SSEG registration) and maintenance of the installed base. Water is the re-emerging driver: dams were ~17 points lower y/y in Sept 2026 and the City was weighing restrictions, while water tariff structures were upended by a court ruling.

### Inferences
- New solar installation is a cooled, oversupplied category (many installers chasing 20-35% of peak demand). The growing niches are downstream of the ~13k+ authorised (and many more unregistered) Cape Town systems: SSEG registration/legalisation, CoC sign-off, panel cleaning, inverter/battery repair and replacement, system expansion, "orphaned system" servicing where the installer has closed. FNB's "trust" barrier suggests a quality gap.
- Tariff-driven efficiency (heat pumps, solar geysers, geyser timers/blankets) is a logical growth niche but I found no numbers.
- If restrictions return in summer 2026/27, expect a repeat of the 2017 spike in wellpoint/borehole, rainwater tank, greywater and pool-cover/backwash-recovery searches; existing boreholes from 2017-18 are now 8-9 years old, implying pump service/repair and water-treatment (iron filter) demand.

- No 2025-26 data on borehole/wellpoint/rainwater tank demand, drilling backlogs, or pool backwash; no heat pump/geyser demand data.
- Current (Nov 2026) water restriction level not confirmed.

## 4. Weather and housing stock

### Takeaway
May 2026 cold fronts were classified a national disaster, with roofs blown off, uprooted trees across the metro, blocked drains and a surge in insurance claims; and 2025/26 was described as the Western Cape's worst fire season in decades, with the official fire season extended from four to eight months. Both feed strongly seasonal, urgent-intent service searches.

### Cited Findings

- Coastal corrosion (gates, garage doors, aluminium, aircon units) is a plausible Cape-specific driver but unsourced.

## 5. Crime and security

### Takeaway
Reported residential burglary in Cape Town is actually falling (about -15% y/y in Q3 2025/26) even as violent crime stays high and Cape Town precincts feature on national top-30 lists; I found no hard data on Cape Town security-install demand.

### Cited Findings
- Cape Town recorded 66,921 crimes in Q3 2025/26 (Oct-Dec 2025); residential burglary 2,500 vs 2,926 a year earlier (-14.6%) — [StreetSignal (third-party analysis of SAPS data)](https://streetsignal-live.pages.dev/blog/cape-town-crime-trends-q3-2025-2026/) (not an official source; verify against SAPS)
- Western Cape residential burglary 5,445, down 12.1%, Jan-Mar 2025 — [Smile FM](https://smilefm.co.za/western-capes-murder-rate-remains-a-major-concern-for-authorities/)
- Cape Town Central tops the national list for serious crime; Mfuleni, Delft, Mitchells Plain, Kraaifontein, Nyanga also in the national top 30 — [EWN, 3 Mar 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/03/03/cape-town-central-tops-national-crime-list-as-serious-offences-rise)
- Western Cape murder rate down 3.4% in Q3 2025/26 — [SA Government](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/western-cape-province-34-decrease-murder-rate-20-feb-2026)
- Criminals adapting to bypass electric fences; advice is layered systems (alarms, cameras, lighting, response) — [BusinessTech, Nov 2025](https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=842159)
- National market-research estimates (low reliability, paywalled vendors): SA smart home security ~US$167m in 2025, ~10.5% CAGR to 2034, cameras the largest product share — [Deep Market Insights](https://deepmarketinsights.com/vista/insights/smart-home-security-market/south-africa)

### Inferences
- Security demand in Cape Town is likely steady rather than surging, and driven by perception, new-owner installs after property transfer (see section 1), electric-fence compliance certificates on sale, and technology upgrades (Wi-Fi/AI cameras, smart alarms) rather than rising burglary counts.
- Gate/garage motor repair, electric fence repair and CCTV upgrades are repeat/maintenance categories; storm and coastal corrosion add to gate-motor failures (inference).

### Gaps
- No Cape Town/Western Cape data on alarm, CCTV, electric fencing, gate motor or burglar-bar demand or provider counts. No armed-response subscriber data.

## 7. City service delivery gaps pushing residents to private providers

### Takeaway
The City clears a sewer blockage roughly every eight minutes (90,000-110,000 a year) and spends R137m-R228m a year doing so; dumping and blockages are chronic, some overflows persist for a year, and extortion disrupts waste collection in some areas. Private drain-unblocking, rubble removal and tree work fill the response-time gap.

### Cited Findings
- 2024/25: 110,598 sewer blockages attended across 9,000+ km of sewer network at a cost of R228m; up to 60% preventable (misuse/dumping); "one cleared every eight minutes" — [City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/Media-and-news/Sewer%20blockages%20cleared%20every%20eight%20minutes%20costs%20City%20millions); [Nova News](https://novanews.co.za/city-fights-blockages-costing-millions/)
- A City release dated 27 Aug 2026 reports 90,002 blockages for 2025/26, described as +2,000 y/y and 80%+ preventable — [City of Cape Town media](https://www.capetown.gov.za/Media-and-news/Ramped%20up%20efforts%20keep%20City's%20487%20sewer%20stations%20pumping) (exact release not verified); note this is inconsistent with the 110,598 figure above (different definitions or a reporting error; unresolved).
- City spent R137m clearing preventable blockages caused by dumping; dumping fines up 60% to 20,000+ cases (financial year unclear) — [News24, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/crime-and-courts/extortion-threat-hits-rubbish-collection-in-cape-towns-informal-settlements-20260421-1066) (attribution to this article not verified); same article: extortion threats hitting rubbish collection in informal settlements.
- "One year later, sewage is still running through some Cape Town streets" — [EWN, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/09/one-year-later-sewage-is-still-running-through-some-cape-town-streets)
- ~150 tonnes of waste removed from Kleinvlei Canal in Nov-Dec 2025 — [City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/Media-and-news/150%20tonnes%20of%20waste%20removed%20from%20Kleinvlei%20Canal)
- City has 487 sewer pump stations — [City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/Media-and-news/Ramped%20up%20efforts%20keep%20City's%20487%20sewer%20stations%20pumping)
- The City runs a plumbers register but "cannot guarantee the validity of the information" in it — [City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/Family%20and%20home/residential-utility-services/residential-water-and-sanitation-services/Find-a-qualified-registered-plumber-in-Cape-Town)

### Inferences
- The City only handles blockages on the municipal side; private-side blockages (the property owner's responsibility) and the volume of public-side incidents sustain high urgent-intent searches for "blocked drain"/"drain unblocking"/"plumber near me" - likely among the highest-volume emergency service queries in Cape Town.
- Rubble/garden-refuse/bulky-waste removal ("bakkie hire", "rubble removal") is fed by limited drop-off capacity and renovation activity; rising dumping fines increase willingness to pay for legitimate removal (inference; no demand data).
- Tree maintenance on verges/private land after storms is another private fallback (see section 4).

### Gaps
- No data on City response times for tree, pothole, bulky waste or streetlight requests (C3 service request stats not retrieved).
- No data on private rubble-removal/drain-cleaning market size or provider quality.
