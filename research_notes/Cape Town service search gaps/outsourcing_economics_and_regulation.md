# Outsourcing economics, feasibility and regulation of subcontracted / lead-gen local service businesses in Cape Town (2025-2026)

> **Method caveat (read first).** In this session direct page fetches were blocked (procompare.co.za, kandua.com, support.google.com and others did not resolve), so every finding below comes from web-search result summaries, not from a full read of the page. Numbers are as reported by the search summaries; where the summary did not say which of several result URLs a number came from, this is flagged "(URL attribution uncertain)". Most price sources are lead-gen aggregators or contractor marketing pages, not audited data. Nothing in "Cited Findings" is my own estimate; all my estimates are confined to "Inferences" and labelled as such.

## 1. Typical price per job / average ticket in Cape Town (ZAR) per category

### Takeaway
Sourced tickets cluster in three bands: small call-out jobs of roughly R450-R2,500 (locksmith, mobile mechanic, appliance repair, garden visit, rubble load, skip, pest treatment), mid jobs of R2,500-R15,000 (geyser replacement, gate motor, aircon, deep clean, tree felling, local move), and projects of R40,000+ (borehole, solar, roofing/waterproofing/paving priced per m2). Cape Town-specific figures were found for only some categories; several (mobile valet, septic pumping, CCTV, 5kW solar, drain unblocking, glass, Airbnb turnover cleans) had no usable sourced price.

## 2. What contractors pay for leads: platform fees, commissions, lead prices

### Takeaway
The only hard South African numbers found are pay-per-lead prices on shared-lead marketplaces: Snupit at R24-R30 per credit and 1-5 credits per lead (R24-R150), and Procompare at about R40-R150 per lead. No sourced figure was found for a standard percentage referral commission between a middleman and a subcontractor.

### Cited Findings
- Snupit: one credit costs R30, falling to R24 in bulk packs; leads cost 1-5 credits depending on category and demand; pros pay only when they buy a lead; "Hero Leads" 30-day add-on exists — [Snupit Pro FAQ](https://www.snupit.co.za/faq-pro.aspx); [Snupit terms](https://www.snupit.co.za/terms-and-conditions.aspx)
- Snupit app bundles: R249.99 for 10 credits, R599.99 for 25, R999.99 for 50 — [Snupit on App Store](https://apps.apple.com/za/app/snupit/id1318715377)
- Procompare: pros pay for the lead, customers pay for the work; most leads R40-R150 (promotional content; URL attribution uncertain between the two) — [News24 partner content](https://www.news24.com/brandstory/partner-content/the-easiest-way-to-get-local-clients-youd-be-surprised-20220214); [Press Portal 2021](https://pressportal.co.za/advertising-and-marketing/story/procompare-generates-top-quality-customer-leads-for-local-businesses.html)
- Procompare pros are on subscription packages managed through an account manager — [Procompare Pro FAQ](https://www.procompare.co.za/faq-pros)
- Kandua is described as using "commission or matching fees" and Bark as per-lead credits (competitor marketing page, unverified) — [ServiceLink SA platform comparison](https://www.servicelinksa.co.za/best-platforms-contractors-find-work-south-africa/)
- A SA agency guide puts cost per lead at R15-R800 across industries; home services R30-R80 per lead via Google Ads and R10-R30 via SEO; SEO takes 3-6 months to produce consistent leads (agency blog, not audited) — [RaimondWeb lead-gen guide](https://raimondweb.pages.dev/insights/lead-generation-south-africa-guide)

### Inferences
- Snupit and Procompare sell each lead to several pros, so R24-R150 is the price of a shared, unqualified lead. An exclusive, phone-qualified lead should command more, but I found no SA source that quantifies the premium.
- (Estimate) If a subcontractor paid 10-15% of job value, a lead that books would be worth roughly R60-R135 on a R600-R900 lockout, R1,000-R2,000 on a geyser or gate motor, and R5,000+ on a borehole. The 10-15% rate is an assumption, not a sourced SA norm.
- Contractors are already conditioned to pay per lead on these platforms, so selling leads is a familiar proposition; the anchor price they will compare against is R40-R150.

### Gaps
- Kandua's actual commission rate, SweepSouth's take rate from cleaners, and Bark's South African credit prices were not found.
- No sourced evidence of what percentage referral fee Cape Town contractors accept from a private lead seller.
- No SA pay-per-call price data found.

## 3. Regulated trades: who must do or sign off the work

### Takeaway
Electrical, plumbing/geyser, gas, pest control, security (alarms/CCTV/locksmith), electric fencing, solar PV, boreholes and waste transport all carry a registration or sign-off requirement, so a middleman must use a registered subcontractor and cannot self-certify. Categories for which I found no licensing requirement in this research include cleaning, carpet cleaning, garden services, pool cleaning, painting, paving, tiling, handyman work, movers, car valet and appliance repair (absence of evidence, not a legal clearance).

### Cited Findings
**Electrical**
- Only a "registered person" holding a wireman's licence registered with the Department of Employment and Labour may inspect, test and issue an electrical Certificate of Compliance; classes include single-phase tester, installation electrician and master installation electrician — [Empowr wireman's licence](https://www.empowr.co.za/compliance/wiremans-licence); [Electrical CoC explained](https://electriciancalloutrate.co.za/guides/electrical-certificate-of-compliance-explained/)
- Passing the trade test makes someone a qualified electrician; the wireman's licence is a separate step, and registration as an electrical contractor is a further step needed to obtain CoC documents — [TDMI wireman's licence guide](https://www.tdmi.co.za/blogs/informative/how-to-register-for-your-wireman-s-license-everything-you-need-to-know-2025-update)

**Plumbing / geyser**
- Geyser (water-heating) installations require a PIRB Certificate of Compliance, which only a PIRB-registered plumber may issue; tied to SANS 10254 and relevant to insurance — [Kandua geyser compliance](https://kandua.com/home-advice/geyser-installation-in-south-africa-ensuring-safety-efficiency-and-compliance); [Kwikot geyser compliance](https://www.kwikot.com/geyser-complaince-in-south-africa)
- Sources conflict on whether a plumbing CoC is required at property transfer: one says it is not a nationwide requirement, another says many municipalities require it — [Kandua plumbing CoC when selling](https://kandua.com/home-advice/selling-your-home-why-and-when-you-might-need-a-plumbing-coc-in-south-africa); [MyProperty compliance guide](https://www.myproperty.co.za/news/legal/your-guide-to-compliance-certificates-as-a-home-owner-08-09-23)

**Gas**
- Gas installations need a gas CoC from a practitioner registered with SAQCC Gas (mandated by the Department of Employment and Labour); a PIRB plumbing qualification alone is not sufficient; a new prescribed gas certificate format applied from 1 May 2023 — [Kandua gas geyser installation](https://kandua.com/home-advice/installing-a-gas-geyser-in-south-africa-key-considerations-for-safety-compliance-and-longevity)

**Pest control**
- Pest control operators must be registered under section 3 of the Fertilizers, Farm Feeds, Agricultural Remedies and Stock Remedies Act 36 of 1947 with the Registrar (Department of Agriculture); the NQF 4 FET Certificate in Pest Control Operations is a prerequisite; employers require the "P-number" — [SME South Africa](https://smesouthafrica.co.za/how-to-start-a-pest-control-business); [SAQA qualification 57830](https://regqs.saqa.org.za/viewQualification.php?id=57830)
- Registration validity is reported inconsistently: three years per a 2011 gazette notice vs 12 months per a third-party site — [Gazette notice](https://old.dalrrd.gov.za/doaDev/sideMenu/ActNo36_1947/gg34020_nn98Fertilizer.pdf); [Wikiprocedure](https://www.wikiprocedure.com/index.php/South_Africa_-_Registration_of_Pest_Control_Operators)

**Security: alarms, CCTV, access control, locksmiths**
- PSIRA is the mandatory regulator for security service providers; alarm and CCTV installers and access-control installers are listed as falling under its remit; "Locksmith Services" is a PSIRA certificate class (procurement-compliance blog, not PSIRA itself) — [Tenders-SA PSIRA electronic security clarification](https://www.tenders-sa.org/blog/security-procurement-guide-2026-03-26)

**Electric fencing**
- Under the Electrical Machinery Regulations 2011, regulation 12(4), every user or lessor of an electric fence system must hold an electric fence system certificate, issued only by a person registered as an electric fence system installer with the Department of Labour; systems predating the regulations are exempt unless altered or the property changes hands — [Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr alert 2014](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/export/sites/cdh/news/publications/2014/real-estate/downloads/Real-Estate-Alert-3-March-2014.pdf); [Nemtek on EF CoC](https://www.nemtek.co.za/?p=19909)
- SABS updates in June 2023 tightened electric fence requirements — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/property/830369/a-warning-to-south-african-homeowners-with-electric-fences/)

**Solar PV (City of Cape Town)**
- Since 1 October 2023 all new solar PV and/or battery systems are treated as grid-tied, must use City-approved inverters, have professional sign-off and be authorised before switching on; off-grid/standby systems are no longer permitted on properties with City electrical connections; electrical contractors and ECSA-registered professionals must register as service providers — [City of Cape Town SSEG application](https://www.capetown.gov.za/applyforsseg)
- Cape Town reportedly retains the Pr.Eng/Pr.Tech sign-off even though Eskom dropped it for residential systems (secondary source) — [Solar-training.org](https://solar-training.org/?p=11551)

**Boreholes (City of Cape Town)**
- Must apply to the City at least 14 days before sinking a borehole or wellpoint and register it afterwards; official registration signage must be displayed — [City: apply to sink a borehole](https://www.capetown.gov.za/City-Connect/Apply/Municipal-services/Water-and-sanitation/Apply-to-sink-a-borehole-or-wellpoint-or-use-an-alternative-source-of-water); [City: register a borehole](https://www.capetown.gov.za/City-Connect/Register/Business-and-trade/Register-a-borehole)

**Waste / rubble / skips (City of Cape Town)**
- Anyone who transports waste in Cape Town must be accredited by the City under the Integrated Waste Management By-law; waste generators and service providers must have an approved integrated waste management plan (section 10, 2009 by-law) — [City: register as accredited waste service provider](https://www.capetown.gov.za/City-Connect/Register/Business-and-trade/Register-as-an-accredited-waste-services-provider); [City: accredited waste service providers](https://www.capetown.gov.za/work%20and%20business/commercial-utility-services/commercial-solid-waste-services/accredited-waste-service-providers)

**Trees**
- The City's Urban Forest Policy treats trees protected under the National Forests Act 84 of 1998 as protected; protected species may only be trimmed by or under supervision of a qualified person — [City Urban Forest Policy](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Bylaws%20and%20policies/Urban_Forest%20Policy.pdf); [City tree trimming notice](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Forms,%20notices,%20tariffs%20and%20lists/Tree_Trimming_Notice.pdf)
- A commercial blog says applications for significant, heritage or Council-owned trees go to the City and lists milkwood as protected (not authoritative) — [Pro Tree Felling Cape Town blog](https://protreefellingcapetown.pages.dev/blog/tree-felling-permit-cape-town)

### Inferences
- Regulation does not stop a non-tradesperson owning the customer relationship; it means the subcontractor must hold the registration and issue the certificate in their own name. The practical risk is that the middleman's brand is on the invoice while the compliance document is the subcontractor's.
- Security categories (locksmith, CCTV, alarms) are the riskiest for a pure middleman, because PSIRA regulates the business providing the service, which may capture the front company as well as the technician. This needs legal confirmation.
- Lowest regulatory barrier (nothing found in this research): home/deep/Airbnb cleaning, carpet and upholstery cleaning, garden services, pool cleaning, painting, paving, tiling, handyman, movers, mobile valet, appliance repair, damp proofing and waterproofing (no trade licence found; workmanship liability still applies).
- Rubble removal and skip hire look simple but carry the City waste-accreditation requirement for whoever transports the waste, plus illegal-dumping exposure if a cheap subcontractor dumps.

### Gaps
- Not verified against primary sources: PIRB, PSIRA, SAQCC Gas, DoEL and SAPCA sites could not be read. PSIRA scope over locksmiths/CCTV rests on a procurement blog.
- No results found for asbestos (Asbestos Abatement Regulations 2020, registered asbestos contractors), tow-truck operator regulation in the Western Cape, septic/honeysucker licensing, aircon refrigerant-handling registration (SAQCC Gas refrigeration category), or PV GreenCard status. These remain unanswered.
- Whether white milkwood is on the current DFFE protected list was not confirmed by an authoritative source.

## 4. Legal and tax considerations of the middleman model

### Takeaway
Google's Business Profile rules are the most immediate constraint: virtual offices are ineligible and third-party summaries say lead-generation agents are ineligible, so rank-and-rent listings on Google Maps are suspension-prone. Under the CPA the customer-facing supplier carries the duty to deliver quality service, and VAT registration only becomes compulsory at R2.3 million turnover from 1 April 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Google Business Profile**
- "If your business rents a physical mailing address but doesn't operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, that location isn't eligible"; service-area businesses can't list a virtual office unless staffed during business hours and should hide their address; co-working spaces need signage and own staff; service area should not extend beyond about 2 hours' drive — [Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)
- Third-party summaries state lead-generation agents/companies are not eligible for a Business Profile (wording not confirmed on Google's page in this research) — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/representing-your-business-on-google/); [Blue Corona](https://www.bluecorona.com/blog/google-business-profile-guidelines/)

**Google Ads / Local Services Ads**
- Google Local Services Ads are not available in South Africa (at time of that article) — [Juicy Designs](https://www.juicydesigns.co.za/blog/google-ads-vs-local-services-ads-south-africa/)
- Locksmiths are described as a restricted, high-risk Google category due to lead-gen fraud, with advertiser verification required (US/UK agency blogs) — [Stackmatix locksmith PPC](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/google-ads-for-locksmiths)

**Consumer Protection Act**
- Section 54 gives consumers the right to quality service; the remedy is to have the service redone or receive a partial refund — [CPA s54 text](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/54_consumer_s_rights_to_demand_quality_service.php)
- Section 61 imposes no-fault liability for harm from unsafe or defective goods across the supply chain; the SCA held in Eskom v Halstead-Cleak [2016] ZASCA 150 that it applies only within a supplier-consumer relationship — [MJK Inc CPA commentary](https://mjkinc.co.za/compliance/consumer-protection-act)
- A 2024 National Consumer Tribunal matter against a home-renovation supplier turned on section 54(1) installation standards — [NCC v Home Renovations](https://thencc.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/73_NCC-v-Home-Renovations_NCT-358791-2024-73.2b-002.pdf)

**Tax**
- From 1 April 2026 the compulsory VAT registration threshold rose from R1 million to R2.3 million of taxable supplies per year; voluntary threshold rose from R50,000 to R120,000; the Turnover Tax qualifying ceiling also rose to R2.3 million — [SARS Budget 2026 FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/budget/budget-2026-frequently-asked-questions/)

### Inferences
- If the middleman contracts with the customer and invoices in its own name (white-label model), it is the "supplier" for section 54 and must fund redo/refunds even when the subcontractor did the work. A pure lead-sale model (customer contracts with the tradesperson) shifts that exposure but must not be advertised as if the lead seller were the tradesperson.
- A white-label model books full job value as turnover, so the VAT threshold is reached sooner than under a lead-sale model where only the commission is turnover. At R2.3 million the threshold is high enough that an early-stage operator in either model is unlikely to be forced to register.
- Because Local Services Ads are absent in SA, standard Search ads and organic/Maps ranking are the only Google channels, and a Maps listing requires a real operating location.

### Gaps
- No source found on the CPA "intermediary" definition and disclosure duties as applied to service lead brokers.
- Nothing retrieved on employee vs independent contractor tests (LRA s200A presumption) or public liability insurance costs.
- Google Ads' own policy text on third-party lead aggregators and misrepresentation was not retrieved; South African advertiser-verification specifics unknown.

## 5. Rank-and-rent and pay-per-call: how they work and evidence in South Africa

### Takeaway
I found almost no public evidence of rank-and-rent or pay-per-call operators reporting rand income in South Africa. The only concrete SA artefact is a Flippa sale listing for a roofing lead-gen site claiming 90-120 organic enquiries a month, with no profit disclosed.

### Cited Findings
- A South African roofing lead-gen site was listed for sale claiming #1 rankings and 90-120 organic roofing enquiries monthly with no paid ads; the site was about 7 months old and the monthly profit field was blank (seller claim, unverified) — [Flippa listing 13916033](https://flippa.com/es/13916033-south-african-roofing-lead-gen-site-ranking-1-for-key-terms-and-generating-90-120-organic-roofing-enquiries-monthly-with-no-paid-ads)
- Established SA lead marketplaces monetise per lead rather than by renting sites: Snupit R24-R150 per lead, Procompare R40-R150 — [Snupit Pro FAQ](https://www.snupit.co.za/faq-pro.aspx); [News24 partner content](https://www.news24.com/brandstory/partner-content/the-easiest-way-to-get-local-clients-youd-be-surprised-20220214)
- Search results surfaced many single-niche, single-city microsites on free hosting (for example skip hire Cape Town, rubble removal Roodepoort, tree felling Cape Town, Pretoria towing) with suburb-by-suburb landing pages — [Skip Hire Cape Town](https://skip-hire-capetown-za.pages.dev/skip-hire-prices-cost/); [Rubble Removal Roodepoort](https://rubbleremovalroodepoort.pages.dev/pricing); [Pro Tree Felling Cape Town](https://protreefellingcapetown.pages.dev/blog/tree-felling-permit-cape-town); [Pretoria Towing](https://pretoriatowing.pages.dev/long-distance-towing-pretoria)

### Inferences
- The suburb-page microsites have the hallmark structure of rank-and-rent or lead-gen sites (one niche, one city, pages per suburb, a single phone number), which suggests the model is being actively attempted in SA, including in Cape Town skip hire and tree felling. Whether they earn money is unknown.
- (Estimate) Using the Flippa claim of 90-120 enquiries/month and the Procompare/Snupit anchor of R40-R150 per lead, such a site would gross roughly R3,600-R18,000 per month if every enquiry were sold once. This is arithmetic on unverified inputs, not reported revenue.
- With no SA case studies, US rank-and-rent income claims (typically quoted in dollars per site per month) should not be translated to rand; SA lead prices are an order of magnitude lower than US CPLs (R40-R150 vs about $91).

### Gaps
- No Reddit, MyBroadband, YouTube or blog case study with SA rental or pay-per-call revenue was found. One targeted search returned only unrelated plumbing job listings.
- No data on SA call-tracking/pay-per-call infrastructure pricing or adoption.

## 6. Subcontractor supply in Cape Town: abundant vs scarce

### Takeaway
No source quantifies contractor supply by trade in Cape Town. Indirect evidence (dense marketplace listings, very low advertised call-out fees) points to abundant supply in handyman, appliance repair, cleaning, garden, rubble, pest and plumbing, while regulated sign-off roles are structurally scarcer.

## 7. Conversion math: clicks to leads to booked jobs

### Takeaway
The best available benchmarks are US: about 7.3% click-to-lead conversion for home services search ads and about 41.5% lead-to-booked-job for plumbing. Combined with an SA agency estimate of R8-R15 per click for plumbing/electrical keywords, that implies a Google Ads cost per lead near R110-R205 and a cost per booked job near R265-R495, although another SA agency claims R30-R80 per lead.

### Cited Findings
- LocaliQ 2025 home services search benchmarks (US): average CPC $7.85, average conversion rate 7.33%, average cost per lead $90.92 — [LocaliQ home services benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-advertising-benchmarks/)
- Plumbing (US): average CPC $10.49, CTR 4.97%, conversion rate 7.63%; another source claims 12-16% conversion; Google Ads CPL $129.02 with lead-to-booked-job rate 41.5%; non-branded plumbing campaigns $183 per lead (agency blogs; URL attribution uncertain) — [Webtonic plumbing statistics](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/plumbing-ad-creative-statistics); [ClicksGeek plumbing benchmarks](https://clicksgeek.com/google-ads-performance-benchmarks-plumbing/)
- Locksmith (US): average CPC $6.20; LSA cost per lead $34 — [Webtonic locksmith statistics](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/locksmith-google-ads-statistics)
- South Africa: home-service keywords such as plumbing and electrical cost roughly R8-R15 per click (agency illustration, not audited) — [RaimondWeb SEO vs Google Ads SA](https://raimondweb.pages.dev/insights/seo-vs-google-ads-south-africa)
- South Africa: Google Ads CPCs generally R10-R150 depending on industry (forum post by a consultant, unverified) — [Google product forum post](https://groups.google.com/g/googleproductforums/c/bM-KK1WLV5I)
- South Africa: home services cost R30-R80 per lead via Google Ads, R10-R30 via SEO (agency blog) — [RaimondWeb lead-gen guide](https://raimondweb.pages.dev/insights/lead-generation-south-africa-guide)
- A first SA Google Search CPC/CTR industry benchmark exists but trade rows were not visible — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/first-sa-google-search-cpc-and-ctr-industry-benchmark-626811a)

### Inferences
- (Derived) CPL = CPC / conversion rate. R8 / 7.33% = R109; R15 / 7.33% = R205. Cost per booked job = CPL / 41.5% = R263 to R493. These mix an SA CPC with US conversion rates.
- The agency's R30-R80 CPL would require either CPCs of about R2-R6 at 7.3% conversion or conversion rates of 10-50% at R8-R15 CPC; it is internally inconsistent with its own CPC figure unless conversion is well above US norms. Treat R30-R80 as optimistic.
- (Derived) At R265-R495 acquisition cost per booked job, paid ads cannot be recovered from a 10-15% commission on tickets under about R2,500-R5,000. Small-ticket categories (locksmith day rates, garden visits, rubble loads, appliance call-outs) only work on paid traffic if the owner keeps the whole margin (white-label), sells the lead to several pros, or the customer recurs. Mid-ticket categories (geyser, gate motor, aircon, tree felling, deep clean, pest contracts) clear the bar.
- (Estimate, overall ranking for a non-tradesperson owner, combining ticket size, regulatory burden, subcontractor supply and acquisition cost; this is judgement, not sourced):
  - Strongest fit: tree felling, deep/move-out/Airbnb cleaning and carpet cleaning, waterproofing/damp proofing/roof painting, painting and paving/tiling projects, gate and garage motors, pool services (recurring), garden services (recurring only).
  - Good economics but must use a registered subcontractor who signs off: geyser replacement (PIRB CoC), plumbing and blocked drains, electrical (CoC), aircon, pest control (P-registered), electric fencing (EF certificate).
  - Workable but thin or risky: rubble removal and skip hire (low ticket, City waste accreditation, dumping liability), handyman and appliance repair (low ticket, variable quality), movers (damage liability), mobile mechanic and valet (low ticket; no price data for valet).
  - Weakest fit for a pure middleman: locksmith, CCTV and alarms (PSIRA, Google scrutiny of locksmith lead-gen), solar (ECSA sign-off, City authorisation, long sales cycle), borehole (capital-heavy drillers, City application), towing and septic pumping (truck owners control supply; no price or regulation data found), gas work (SAQCC).

### Gaps
- No SA-specific click-to-lead or lead-to-booking benchmark exists in the sources found; no Cape Town Keyword Planner CPCs by trade were obtained.
- Lead-to-job booking rates for trades other than plumbing were not found.
- Call-versus-form split and phone answer rates for SA local services are unknown.
